# PRD Quality Review — SlotBook

## Overall verdict

The SlotBook PRD is decisive and unusually concrete. It has a five-problem thesis that the features really do trace to, a glossary used with discipline, numeric bounds on almost every rule, named concurrency tests, an honest non-goals list, and every assumption closed with its reasoning logged. The risk sits in a few core rules where an agent will have to guess. "Patient name" appears in four FRs but is never collected. Ticket lookups are undefined once a code is reused or a booking is final. FR-14's "refused only if" contradicts the capacity and closed-slot rules. And the sequence number breaks its own glossary range after one cancellation. Fix those four before `bmad-ticket` slices stories. Close the medium findings in the same pass, or carry them into the affected stories as explicit decisions.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions, not as considerations: "It is loose on purpose, because mobile carriers put many phones behind one public IP" (FR-3), "Chat replies never go by SMS" (FR-37), "A published week can't go back to draft" (FR-8), "Reserved means reserved" (FR-14). Trade-offs name what was given up. Examples are the addendum's *Rejected alternatives* table, the recorded reasons for dropping email OTP, and SM-C1, which trades SMS savings against identity strength. The open questions are really open and each has an owner: Q1 and Q4 go to architecture, Q3 and Q5 to before launch. Each struck-through question shows its resolution and the FR it landed in. The one `[NOTE FOR PM]` (NFR-8) sits at a real handoff.

The gaps are three tensions that the PRD creates but doesn't name. Each one will surface later as a surprise rather than a choice.

### Findings

- **medium** SMS budget contention is unnamed (FR-3, FR-27–FR-29, FR-33, NFR-16, NFR-17, Q1). The daily OTP budget caps OTP sends only. Confirmations, reminders and announcement fan-out ("all patients with an active booking", FR-33) draw on the same SMS quota with no cap and no priority. One announcement can therefore use up the free quota and push the rest of the day into NFR-17's degraded mode ("new devices can't sign in"). Bangla and Arabic SMS also use Unicode encoding (70 characters per segment), so a notification that carries practitioner, date, time and code is probably two segments. The Q1 cost sizing needs that number. *Fix:* add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` to Q1 with the per-category SMS volume at first-tenant sizing (about 300 confirmations and 300 reminders a day, plus OTP and fan-out, counted in Unicode segments). Also state a priority rule, for example "announcements never starve OTP or booking notifications".
- **medium** The practitioner-booking exemption is broader than the recorded decision (FR-14, FR-17, §3 *Practitioner booking*). `.memlog.md` C4 records that "practitioner-made bookings **into reserved places** do not count". The PRD exempts every practitioner booking, including bookings into free places ("into a reserved or free place", FR-14; "Practitioner bookings (FR-14) are not counted", FR-17). That gives staff a limit-free path into public capacity, which touches the hoarding problem the product exists to solve. *Fix:* either confirm the broader rule and log it in `.memlog.md`, or narrow the exemption to reserved places.
- **medium** The "one official booking address" has no decision against the $0 constraint (§4.7, NFR-16). Anti-impersonation depends on one recognisable address. NFR-16 says that any component without a free tier needs a free alternative. A custom domain has no free tier, and a shared-platform subdomain is easy to imitate. The PRD never says which one the clinic gets. *Fix:* either name the domain as the one pre-sale exception in NFR-16, or accept a platform subdomain and record the weaker anti-impersonation as a known trade-off.
- **low** Retention (Q3) will collide with the append-only audit log (FR-42, NFR-11). "No role can edit or delete an entry", together with the addendum's plan to remove UPDATE and DELETE rights, leaves no way to purge audit entries once Q3 sets a retention period. *Fix:* add one consequence to FR-42: once a retention period exists, purging entries older than it is the only deletion allowed. A separate maintenance identity does it, and each purge is itself logged.

## Substance over theater — strong

There are three roles and eight UJs, and each UJ drives specific FRs: UJ-2 drives FR-12 and FR-13, UJ-5 drives FR-14, UJ-7 drives FR-26 and FR-32. The Vision couldn't be moved to another booking product. It names "a QR code plus a short code that the practitioner checks at the door", the five problems, and the rule that every control maps to one of them. The NFRs carry thresholds (95% of availability reads under 500 ms at about 100 concurrent patients, 360 px, WCAG 2.2 AA, a restore tested before launch). §1 says openly that the product is also a learning vehicle, and the addendum's messaging section shows that those jobs are real. There is very little furniture.

### Findings

- **low** NFR-10 and NFR-11a are boilerplate (§5.3). NFR-10, "encrypted in transit and at rest", and NFR-11a, "stored encrypted at rest along with the rest of the data", add nothing testable. NFR-11a's only real content is "excluded from logs". *Fix:* turn the log exclusion into a testable consequence, for example "a log scan in the test suite finds no reason category, age or note", and drop the restatement.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is clear: phone booking causes five problems, and "Every control in it maps to one of these." The features do trace back to it:

- reserved places address missed bookings
- OTP identity and the booking limit address hoarding
- the ticket and the door check address false claims at the door
- official contacts, the home warning, the public check and fraud reports address impersonation
- web booking and chat address the phone bottleneck

The §7.2 slicing follows dependencies, not ease. The weak point is §8. SM-1 to SM-5 are test-suite gates: valuable, but they prove the build, not the thesis. Only SM-10 measures a problem going away. The MVP is "everything in §4" with no launch line.

### Findings

- **medium** Post-launch metrics cover one problem out of five (§8). SM-1 to SM-5 and SM-7 to SM-9 are pre-launch test or demo gates. SM-10, "booking calls drop by at least half within 4 weeks", is the only outcome metric, and it covers only the phone bottleneck. After launch, nothing tells the developer whether missed patients are being fitted in, whether hoarding fell, whether door disputes stopped, or whether impersonation is being caught. *Fix:* add 2–3 outcome SMs computed from data v1 already records (the audit log and the FR-40 CSV). Examples: practitioner bookings into reserved places per week for missed bookings, and *Not genuine* door checks plus fraud reports per week for door claims and impersonation.
- **medium** SM-2 can't be tested as written (§8 SM-2; FR-24, FR-26). SM-2 says wrong-number tickets are rejected "at the door check and the public check". The door check takes no number, though: UJ-4 relies on the practitioner asking for the last three digits. And the public check answers a wrong-day ticket with "Genuine for ⟨date⟩", which is not a rejection. *Fix:* split SM-2 by the cases each check really covers. Door check: forged, reused, wrong-day and cancelled. Public check: forged, wrong-number and cancelled, plus whatever FR-26 decides for used and lapsed tickets.
- **medium** There is no launch line inside the 44 FRs (§7.1, §7.2). §7.1 puts "Everything in §4, FR-1 to FR-44 … plus the NFRs" in v1, and §7.2's slicing is explicitly "not requirements". A live clinic is waiting and an agent is building, yet nothing says which epics must ship for go-live and which can follow within weeks. Chat, announcements and fraud reports are the obvious candidates. Their inclusion is decided; only their timing is open. *Fix:* mark each §7.2 epic as *go-live* or *fast-follow*, one word each.
- **low** The counter-metrics have no way to be measured (§8 SM-C1 to SM-C4). OTP skip rate, booking volume per number and free places at publication aren't shown anywhere, and FR-40 rules out analytics. SM-C3 and SM-C4 also "counterbalance" FRs rather than SMs, and it's unclear why SM-C2 is paired with SM-6. *Fix:* say where each one is read (an audit-log query, the CSV or the logs), or drop the ones nobody will look at.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

Most FRs are excellent. FR-2, FR-3, FR-15, FR-17 and FR-25 carry numbers, error codes and named concurrency tests ("200 concurrent patients competing for 10 places end with exactly 10 bookings"), and adjectives are rare. The failures sit in the rules that the core features depend on: patient identity, ticket lookup, practitioner booking and sequence numbers. An agent will settle each of them silently, in one direction or the other. The medium findings are mostly unstated edge rules and lists that disagree with each other.

### Findings

- **high** "Patient name" is used but never collected (FR-11, FR-35, FR-41; §3 *Patient*, *Attendee*; FR-16, FR-44). FR-11 lists "patient name", FR-35 shows "the patient's name", and FR-41 lets staff "find a patient by mobile number or name" and "shows the patient's name". But the glossary gives a patient only a verified mobile number, FR-16 collects only the *attendee's* name, and FR-44's erasure list has no patient name. A patient who chats before booking (UJ-8) or books for a child has no name of their own. The agent will invent a field or reuse the last attendee name. *Fix:* pick one. (a) Patients have no name: FR-11, FR-35 and FR-41 show the masked number plus the attendee names of the patient's bookings, and search is by number or attendee name. (b) The patient gives a display name at first sign-in: add it to FR-16 (or FR-1), the glossary and FR-44's erasure list.
- **high** Ticket lookup is undefined for final and reused codes (FR-22, FR-24, FR-26; UJ-4, UJ-7). FR-22 lets "codes of final bookings" be reused, and checking in makes a booking final, so a code can be issued again on the same day. FR-24's *Not genuine* means "no such active or recent ticket", but "recent" is never defined. When a typed code matches one active booking and several final ones, nothing says which result wins, and UJ-4's "Already used at 09:14" depends on that. FR-26 answers "only" *Genuine for ⟨date⟩*, *Cancelled* or *Not genuine*, so a `checked_in` or `lapsed` ticket has no defined answer, and the buyer of a used ticket (UJ-7) needs one. *Fix:* (1) Restrict reuse: a code is unique among all bookings whose slot date is today or later, whatever their state, and becomes reusable only after that date. (2) Define "recent" as that same window. (3) State the precedence: the active booking first, then the most recent final one. (4) Map `checked_in` and `lapsed` explicitly in FR-26.
- **high** FR-14's refusal rule contradicts capacity and closed slots, and leaves the kind of place open (FR-14; FR-11, FR-15, FR-18). Read literally, "it is refused only if the patient already has a booking in the same slot" allows a practitioner booking into a full or closed slot. That contradicts FR-11 ("accepts no new booking of any kind") and FR-15 ("capacity in total"). FR-14 also leaves three things open. First, when a slot has both kinds of place, who decides whether the booking uses a reserved one or a free one? FR-18 depends on it ("reserved again, if it was a practitioner booking made into a reserved place"). Second, can a practitioner book into a slot that has already started, such as a same-day walk-in? Third, which failure reasons does it return? *Fix:* rewrite as: "Refused if the slot is closed, if the kind of place the practitioner chose (reserved or free) has none left, or if the patient already holds a booking in the slot. The booking limit never refuses it." Then add the started-slot rule and list the failure reasons, as FR-15 does.
- **high** The sequence number contradicts its own definition after a cancellation (§3 *Sequence number*; FR-15, FR-18, FR-10). The glossary says "position within its slot (1…capacity), given in booking order", and FR-15 says "the next sequence number". Take a slot with capacity 12 and 12 bookings: after one cancellation and one new booking, the new number is either 13 (outside 1…capacity) or a reused number (not booking order). A naive "count + 1" would even duplicate a live number. Lowering capacity (FR-10) after numbers are issued has the same problem. In a walk-in window the number is the patient's place in the queue, so a duplicate shows up at the door. *Fix:* choose one rule and put it in the glossary. Either numbers are never reused and may exceed capacity, or a new booking takes the lowest free number. Add a test that two live bookings in one slot never share a number.
- **medium** Owner alerts have no defined place to appear (FR-3, FR-27, NFR-2, NFR-17; §3 *Notification*). The PRD says "the owner is alerted" (FR-3, NFR-17), "recorded as failed and visible to the owner" (FR-27) and "reports any drift to the owner" (NFR-2). The glossary defines a Notification as a message "to a patient", and no FR says how an owner is alerted or where failed notifications are listed. *Fix:* add an "Owner alerts" FR. It should list the alert kinds (OTP budget used up, SMS quota exhausted, notification failed, counter drift), say where alerts appear (for example a persistent banner on staff pages plus an alert list), say whether any go by SMS (they would draw on the exhausted quota), and say when an alert clears.
- **medium** For a late arrival, check-in depends on when the lapse job happens to run (FR-21, FR-24, FR-25). A booking "becomes `lapsed` automatically within 15 minutes" of slot end, and check-in needs a *Genuine* result. A patient who arrives 5 minutes after their slot ends may or may not be checkable, depending on job timing, so any test of it is non-deterministic. *Fix:* state the rule from the clock, for example "a `confirmed` booking can be checked in until ⟨slot end + N minutes⟩; after that the door check returns *Lapsed*, whether or not the job has run".
- **medium** The PRD doesn't say what a known-device skip grants, or what sign-out forgets (FR-4, FR-5, FR-32; §3 *Known device*). The glossary and FR-4 talk about "a booking from a known device", but the addendum says the skip "grants a session for that number". It's unclear whether a session obtained that way satisfies FR-32's "Reports require a patient session (OTP)". FR-4 names "sign out everywhere" as a way to revoke, but no FR defines it. And FR-5's plain sign-out doesn't say whether the device stays known. On a shared family phone, the next user could type the number and get in for 90 days. *Fix:* state whether a skip creates a normal FR-5 session (recommended: valid for everything except FR-44), define "sign out everywhere" in FR-5, and say whether signing out on a device also forgets it as a known device.
- **medium** The draft-week lifecycle and copying are underspecified (FR-7; UJ-1). UJ-1 says the draft "is already there" and that "the empty days were copied". FR-7 says a draft "can be pre-filled by copying the previous week's slots" and "never overwrites slots already in the draft". Four things are unstated. (1) How a draft week comes into existence, and how far ahead drafts can exist. (2) Who triggers the copy (owner, practitioner or automatic) and at what grain (week, day or practitioner). (3) What happens when a copied slot would overlap an existing one: FR-7 forbids overlaps, and slots have no identity across weeks, so "overwrite" doesn't mean anything. (4) Whether reserved counts and closed status are copied. *Fix:* add those four rules as consequences and make UJ-1 match them.
- **medium** Edits to published slots and date closing are left open (FR-10, FR-11, FR-42). FR-10 covers adding slots and raising or lowering capacity. It doesn't say whether a published slot's date or times can change, or whether the slot can be deleted. FR-42 audits "every slot change after publication", which suggests more changes are possible. FR-11's "close a whole date" doesn't say whether a slot added to that date later starts closed. *Fix:* state "a published slot's date and times can't change and it can't be deleted; close it and add another". Also say whether a closed date is a state or a one-off bulk close.
- **medium** The ticket alphabet size is wrong (FR-22; addendum *Ticket code*). "`A–Z` and `2–9`, excluding `O`, `I` and `L` (27 symbols)" is actually 23 letters plus 8 digits, which is 31 symbols (about 923k codes). It is not 27, which is where the addendum's "about 531k" comes from. No test of the alphabet can satisfy both. *Fix:* either state 31, or name the four further excluded characters, and correct the addendum.
- **medium** The owner/practitioner session separation contradicts the shared endpoints (NFR-4; §3 *Practitioner*; FR-24, FR-25, FR-35, FR-40, FR-41). NFR-4 says "Patient, practitioner and owner sessions are distinct audiences; one can never satisfy another's endpoint." But many endpoints serve "a practitioner or owner", and "An owner may also be a practitioner." Read literally, an owner who also sees patients would need two sessions. *Fix:* "Patient and staff sessions are distinct audiences. Owner and practitioner are roles on a staff account, and one account may hold both. Each endpoint states which roles it accepts."
- **medium** FR-39's settings list misses the setup-time settings and the rules for changing them (FR-39; FR-1; §3 *Week*). The country (FR-1) and the week start day ("chosen when the tenant is set up") aren't listed. The time zone *is* listed, as something an owner can change at any time, yet week boundaries, reminders, the cut-off and the OTP budget's "calendar day" all depend on it. A change to the reminder lead time also has no rule for reminders already scheduled. *Fix:* split the settings into setup-only (country, time zone, week start) and owner-editable. Say whether a changed lead time applies to reminders not yet sent or only to new bookings.
- **medium** Localised digits in input and output are unspecified (FR-1, FR-2, FR-22, FR-26, FR-43). Bangla and Arabic keyboards type `০–৯` and `٠–٩`. Nothing says whether mobile numbers, OTPs and ticket codes accept those digits, and FR-43 ("formatted for the active language") doesn't say whether times, dates and sequence numbers are shown in local digits. Whether a patient who types her number in Bangla digits gets a validation error will depend on what the agent assumed. *Fix:* normalise Bangla and Arabic-Indic digits in all inputs to ASCII. Always show ticket codes, mobile numbers and OTPs in ASCII digits. Decide the digit system for dates, times and sequence numbers in each language.
- **medium** NFR-6 demands rate limits without giving numbers (NFR-6; FR-9, NFR-13). It says "Every public endpoint (… OTP verify, … availability) has a rate limit per IP", but only OTP send (FR-3) and the public check (FR-26) have numbers. FR-3's own argument, that carriers put many phones behind one IP at the publication peak, applies even more to availability reads, the busiest endpoint at that moment (NFR-13). *Fix:* give per-IP numbers for availability and OTP verify, loose for availability for the same NAT reason. Or explicitly hand the numbers to architecture, with that constraint stated.
- **medium** FR-42's audit list is incomplete (FR-42; NFR-9, FR-44, FR-38). "An audit entry is written for:" reads as a complete list, yet it misses the full-number CSV export (NFR-9: "is an audit entry"), patient deletion (FR-44: "Deletion is itself an audit entry") and refused staff actions (FR-38: "refused and recorded"). The audit log is built in the first epic (§7.2), so the agent will build from FR-42's list. *Fix:* add those three and mark the list as a minimum. Consider adding announcement notify sends and fraud-report reviews too.
- **medium** Free-text cancellation reasons put personal data into the immutable log (FR-20, FR-11, FR-42, FR-44). The log records "every cancellation (with the reason)", and the reason is free text typed by staff (1–200 characters) that will often name the patient or their condition. FR-42 promises that an entry "never" contains a name, "so erasure (FR-44) never needs to change one". The reason breaks that promise, and FR-44 doesn't list cancellation reasons among the data it removes. *Fix:* keep the reason text on the booking, where FR-44 erases it, and have the audit entry refer to it. Alternatively, make the reason a category from a tenant-defined list, with optional text stored only on the booking.
- **medium** Erasure leaves sessions and devices working (FR-44). FR-44 doesn't say that the patient's sessions, known-device markers and push subscriptions are revoked. It also doesn't say whether the bookings it cancels trigger cancellation SMS. The number is removed in the same step, but sending is asynchronous (FR-27). *Fix:* add "deletion ends every session, known-device marker and push subscription of that number", and decide whether cancellation notices go out before the number is removed.
- **low** FR-37 contradicts FR-27 on push. FR-27 says "Push is sent in addition to SMS, never instead of it", but chat replies go by push and "never go by SMS" (FR-37). *Fix:* limit FR-27's sentence to the FR-28, FR-29 and FR-33 notifications.
- **low** FR-15's failure reasons are incomplete. The "at most one booking in the same slot" rule has no reason code, and "nearest free slots" isn't defined: by start time, forward only, or within the same week? *Fix:* add a code such as `already_booked` and define "nearest".
- **low** Real-time behaviour has no time bound (FR-34, FR-35). "Appears without a page reload" has no latency, so the addendum's polling fallback would satisfy it at any interval. *Fix:* add a bound, for example "within 10 seconds".
- **low** The CSV export is open to formula injection (FR-40). Attendee names and notes are user-entered text in a file the owner opens in a spreadsheet, and NFR-7 covers only HTML. *Fix:* require cells that begin with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` to be escaped, and list the export's columns.
- **low** The concurrency tests leave out three races (NFR-1). Lowering capacity (FR-10), closing a slot (FR-11) and releasing places (FR-13) can each race a booking, but NFR-1's test list covers only booking, the limit, reserving and check-in. *Fix:* add a named test for each race.
- **low** There's no language fallback for owner-written text (FR-33, FR-36). "Optional text per language" doesn't say what a patient sees in a language with no text. *Fix:* fall back to the tenant's default language.
- **low** FR-16's visibility rule isn't carried into FR-35 and FR-41. The inbox shows any practitioner the patient's "active bookings", and patient lookup shows "their bookings (all states)". Neither says whether the reason category and the note appear for bookings with other practitioners. *Fix:* state that both views follow FR-16.

## Scope honesty — adequate

The non-goals do real work. §6 has 14 explicit exclusions, including rescheduling, a front-desk role, an offline door check, and treatment history (added during reconciliation). All 31 assumptions are resolved, the four open questions are deferred with named owners, and NFR-8 carries its note. The number of open items is low for production stakes. A few omissions are silent, though, and one matters for a live launch.

### Findings

- **medium** There is no privacy notice or consent step (§4.12, NFR-11a, Q3). The product collects health-adjacent data (NFR-11a) and sends SMS to the numbers it holds, and Q3 refers to a "privacy policy". Yet no FR shows a privacy notice or records agreement before the first OTP or booking, and §6 doesn't rule one out. *Fix:* either add an FR (the notice is shown and accepted before the first OTP, the owner maintains the text, and every patient page links to it), or add a non-goal with the reason.
- **low** The PRD doesn't say what happens when a practitioner with future bookings is disabled (FR-38). FR-38 is silent on that practitioner's published slots and `confirmed` bookings. FR-11's close-and-list flow is the obvious answer, but it isn't stated. *Fix:* say either "disabling a practitioner who has future slots is refused until those slots are closed" or "disabling closes them and lists the affected bookings as in FR-11".
- **low** Some smaller omissions are also silent. There's no rule for a patient who changes their mobile number. There are no staff-side notifications (new chat, new booking). And there's no practitioner profile for patients: UJ-3 has Amina pick "Dr. Karim", but nothing says what she sees about him. *Fix:* one line each, either in §6 or as a consequence.

## Downstream usability — strong

The glossary terms are defined and used exactly, with only a few exceptions. FR, NFR, UJ and SM IDs are contiguous and unique, and every cross-reference resolves except the ADR citation (see the mechanical notes). Every feature description lists the UJs it realizes. Every UJ has a named protagonist with context. §7.2 maps FRs to epics, and the addendum keeps the "how" out of the PRD. UX, architecture and `bmad-ticket` can extract from this cleanly once the done-ness highs are fixed.

### Findings

- **low** The glossary has gaps (§3). "Patient name" is used but never defined (see the first high finding). *Notification* covers only patients, although owners get alerts. *Ticket* leaves out the attendee name that FR-23 shows. NFR-3 mentions an "API key" that v1 doesn't have. "Clinic inbox" isn't defined, although *Conversation* is. *Fix:* add or correct these entries.
- **low** UJ-7 skips the sign-in that FR-32 requires (UJ-7, FR-32, NFR-6). Rahim "files a report" straight from the public page, but FR-32 requires an OTP session, while NFR-6 lists "fraud report" among the public endpoints. UX will design from the UJ. *Fix:* add the OTP step to UJ-7, and remove fraud report from NFR-6's public list (or make it public on purpose).

## Shape fit — strong

SlotBook is a multi-role product (patients, practitioners, owners) with meaningful UX, and it sits at the top of a chain (UX → architecture → tickets → sandbox agent). For that shape the UJs carry weight, and they are present and proportionate: eight UJs across three roles. Capability FRs with testable consequences suit an agent build. Mechanism mostly stays in the addendum. The few mechanism statements left in FRs are deliberate, testable invariants, not premature architecture: NFR-1's "enforced by the data store at commit time", FR-42's "the application's own database access must not allow it", and FR-2's "stored only as a hash". Given the stakes, that's acceptable. The PRD is neither over- nor under-formalized. No findings.

## Mechanical notes

- **Dates:** the frontmatter of both the PRD and the addendum says `updated: 2026-10-09`, but the session-008 changes are dated 2026-10-10 (for example the Q6 resolution).
- **Assumptions index roundtrip:** clean. No inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags remain, and every §10 row is resolved. §10's header still says "Each item needs explicit confirmation. **(B)** marks a likely phase-blocker", and no (B) markers remain. §0's description of `[ASSUMPTION]` tags is now historical. Update both lines.
- **Broken reference:** §6 cites ADR-0005 for "Web only". ADR-0005 records the Angular choice; the web-only constraint lives in the brief's *Constraints* section.
- **Numbers:** FR-22 says "27 symbols" for an alphabet of 31 symbols, and the addendum's "about 531k codes" follows the wrong count (see Done-ness).
- **ID series:** NFR-11a is an inserted ID. It works, but NFR-20 would keep the series flat for extraction.
- **Cross-reference by ID:** §4.11 says "Realizes the *Demo-ready* criterion"; cite SM-9.
- **Overclaim:** SM-9 says it "Validates the whole of §4", but its flow doesn't touch chat, announcements, fraud reports or erasure.
- **Singular owner:** "the owner" appears in §3 *Reserved place*, FR-3, NFR-2 and NFR-17, although a tenant can have several owners (Q6). Use "an owner" or "every owner" as meant.
- **Age visibility:** FR-24's *Genuine* result shows the age. NFR-11a says age is "shown only as FR-16 allows", but FR-16's visibility rule covers only the reason category and the note.
- **Dependency:** FR-3's default daily OTP budget waits on Q1. The FR-3 story needs either a placeholder default or to be scheduled after Q1.
